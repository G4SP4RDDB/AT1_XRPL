# Developer Feedback Report — XRPL Lending Protocol Hackathon 2026

**Track** 1, open-ended Single Asset Vault + Lending Protocol V1 · **Flavour** Loaded (XLS-65/66 + native 2-of-2 multisig used as a call-date enforcer) · **Environment** Custom Hackathon Devnet, `rippled 3.4.0-rc1`, NetworkID 4001 · **Libraries** `xrpl.js 5.2.0`, `xrpl-connect 0.8.2` · **Team** BSA Degen (`sunny-puffin-57`)

We built an AT1 bond platform: one bond is one open-ended vault, investors deposit through their own wallet, the deposit that funds the vault originates the loan to the issuer automatically, coupons raise the price per share, investors harvest yield at any time with a partial withdrawal, and principal stays locked until the call date. Seventeen steps of that lifecycle, six of them deliberate rejections, are verified on the devnet (`docs/e2e-full-report.md`). The 30 findings below are logged with repros, severities and versions in `docs/friction-log.md`; each ends with what we would change.

## 1. The headline: the ledger has no notion of *when*, on either side

XLS-65 locks the principal for free while it is out on loan (`VaultWithdraw` fails `tecINSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`, tx `C4240633AC…`), but nothing in XLS-66 stops the issuer from repaying early with `tfLoanFullPayment` and making it liquid before the call date. We closed that with a 2-of-2 signer list on the issuer's account (`SignerListSet`, master disabled with `AccountSet asfDisableMaster`): the issuer's operator key plus an autonomous enforcer daemon that refuses to co-sign a full payment before the call date, read from the validated ledger close time. The ledger enforces the *who* faithfully (`tefMASTER_DISABLED`, `tefBAD_QUORUM`, both in the e2e run) but the *when* lives in our daemon, because a `SignerEntry` has no time condition. The same is needed on the depositor side: the spec gives the vault owner no lever over withdrawals (`VaultWithdraw` "does not respect the permissioned domain rules"; `AssetsMaximum` and `tfVaultPrivate` gate deposits only), so a second 2-of-2 on investor accounts lets the enforcer sign yield-share redemptions and refuse principal until settlement. Two trust assumptions dressed as locks. **Proposals**: an optional `SignAfter` / `SignBefore` on `SignerEntry`, rejected by the engine with a dedicated `tef` code; and an owner-set redemption gate on `VaultSet` (`WithdrawalCapPerPeriod` + `WithdrawalPeriodSeconds`), the quarterly-liquidity-window pattern of real funds. `TokenEscrow` with `FinishAfter` is enabled here and buildable, but escrow release and `LoanPay` stay two unlinked transactions, which is exactly what a signer condition fixes.

## 2. Vaults and loans: spec, codes and docs

There is no `LoanDraw`: `LoanSet` originates and disburses in one transaction. The spec (§3.8.6 item 6) says origination adds `InterestDue` to `AssetsTotal`; on-chain it does not (tx `23AA66A740…`: `AssetsTotal` unchanged at 200,000,000 drops, loan `TotalValueOutstanding` 200,000,762), and we rebuilt our PPS dashboard on that discovery; align the spec or mark the change by amendment. Setting `AssetsMaximum` to the amount raised fails `LoanSet` with `tecLIMIT_EXCEEDED` because the cap must also hold the interest headroom, and the code does not say which cap; a distinct code or an `InterestDueHeadroom` in `vault_info` would. Rates are tenths of a basis point per year with a 60-second minimum interval, so short demo loans earn fractions of a drop and hit `tecPRECISION_LOSS`; a devnet testing note with worked numbers, or an SDK helper computing `PeriodicPayment` and `InterestDue`, would save every team an afternoon.

Closing a loan taught us the most. Once a coupon is overdue, `LoanPay` requires `tfLoanLatePayment`, mutually exclusive with `tfLoanFullPayment`, so a delinquent loan cannot be settled in one transaction (`tecEXPIRED` on our first attempt); let a full payment sweep the overdue amounts. The three flags are tabulated nowhere with their values (`tfLoanOverpayment 0x10000`, `tfLoanFullPayment 0x20000`, `tfLoanLatePayment 0x40000`), and nothing says that at the final due date you simply pay the last coupon and the loan closes by itself, `tfLoanFullPayment` only ever meaning an early close: we published wrong values and mislabelled our own settlement (`EA805215F3…` carries `0x40000`) until we read the enum and the ledger. Fee destinations are scattered too: the early close sent the `CloseInterestRate` penalty to the vault (PPS 1.0000056 → 1.0066) and `ClosePaymentFee` to the broker, `ManagementFeeRate` skims each coupon; one table would do. `InterestRate` is immutable for the life of the loan, while AT1 coupons reset at the call date; a rate reset would be needed for real fixed income. Impairment surprised us twice: with `fixCleanup3_4_0`, `tfLoanImpair` on a current loan returns `tecTOO_SOON` (`E69143C182…`), so a write-down is overdue-triggered, not the discretionary broker action the workshop described, and `tfLoanUnimpair` on a healthy loan returns `tecNO_PERMISSION` (`3CA1EDC1FD…`), which reads as an auth failure; document the precondition, return a "not impaired" code. And first-loss capital has no plain-language lifecycle anywhere (`LoanBrokerCoverDeposit` fills `CoverAvailable`, it absorbs losses before depositors, `LoanSet` is refused below `CoverRateMinimum × DebtTotal`, the broker refills it); a team member had to ask what the top-up button meant.

## 3. Identity and time that a bond needs and the ledger cannot express

A bond vault must lend to its issuer only. Neither `VaultCreate` nor `LoanBrokerSet` can restrict a `LoanSet` counterparty; we store the issuer in the vault's `Data` field and enforce it in the shim and, independently, in the enforcer before it counter-signs. Whitelisting is asymmetric: depositors can be gated natively (`tfVaultPrivate` + `DomainID`, Credentials, both enabled here, the natural home for accredited-investor and minimum-ticket rules, which we did not get to), borrowers cannot, and the depositor gate stops at deposits. **Proposals**: `LoanBrokerSet.DomainID` or `AllowedCounterparty`, and a flag letting a private vault's withdrawals respect the domain. Automatic origination we do by decoding the deposit blob after submission, since there is no "vault funded" hook; a bid's funding window is equally off-chain, so a background scan settles a stalled bond at its deadline (originate above a 50 % floor, otherwise leave it for withdrawal); a deposit deadline on `VaultCreate` would make the window a ledger fact. Finally, when our broker seed leaked into a README for a day we learned that a vault owner or loan broker key cannot be rotated, `VaultCreate` and `LoanBrokerSet` bind to the submitting account; document RegularKey + SignerList from day one, and ship a pre-commit seed guard in the starter kit (the DevEx hook already has the redaction patterns).

## 4. Wallets: `xrpl-connect` on a custom network

WalletConnect (Xaman) is our only signing path, and it took three fixes against defects read in the shipped bundle. The adapter identifies the ledger by a CAIP-2 id and only knows `xrpl:0/1/2`; this network has none, WalletConnect v2 downgrades the requested namespace to optional, the wallet approves the chains it supports, and every request tagged `xrpl:2` is rejected client-side before the wallet sees it (`Failed to sign transaction. Missing or invalid. request() chainId: xrpl:2`). `WalletConnectAdapter.sign()` hardcodes `autofill: true` and returns `tx_json.TxnSignature` as `tx_blob`, the bare signature, unsubmittable. `reconnect()` never resumes the stored session; it proposes a new pairing nobody can approve, so nothing survives a reload. Our `signPrepared()` calls the WalletConnect client directly with a session-approved chain id, `autofill: false` (the backend already autofilled, `NetworkID 4001` included) and `encode(tx_json)`; with that, Xaman signed for NetworkID 4001 without complaint. **Proposals**: chain id from the session namespaces, expose `autofill`, return `encode(tx_json)`, resume sessions, and an `xrpl:<NetworkID>` convention so custom networks exist at all.

Two gaps remain. No adapter can produce a multisig `Signers` entry (the adapter requests `xrpl_signTransactionFor` at pairing but never calls it), so each 2-of-2 account's operator key is generated and held by our backend, the owner's wallet signing only the one-time `SignerListSet` + `AccountSet`. And even a wallet implementing `xrpl_signTransactionFor` could not sign a `LoanSet` as counterparty: `signLoanSetByCounterparty` uses dedicated hash prefixes (`0x43505400`, `0x43504d00`, `fixCleanup3_4_0`) that the spec never mentions; its `multisign` option, given as a string, is also used verbatim as `Signer.Account`, which produced "Duplicate Signers not allowed". **Proposals**: `signForMultisig()` and `signLoanSetAsCounterparty()` adapter primitives, and one sentence in XLS-66 on what the counterparty signature signs. A timing footgun with a clear code: `client.autofill()` sets `LastLedgerSequence` to current + 20, about a minute here; the 2-of-2 activation needs two consecutive approvals in Xaman, so the first transaction came back `tefMAX_LEDGER`; an offset option on `autofill` and a note that the default is sized for immediate submission.

Beyond the defects, mobile-wallet signing on a custom devnet is impractical for iterative work: every signature is a phone round trip, each reload means a new scan, the 2-of-2 is two approvals, and Xaman cannot be pointed at this network (testnet chain id on screen, no balance or history, every test seed typed in by hand). A lifecycle through the UI is five or six phone interactions; a rehearsal takes ten minutes instead of one. Our teammate wrote a local-seed adapter to escape it; we removed it to keep one real-wallet path, the right product decision and the wrong development experience. A clearly labelled developer signer in `xrpl-connect`, custom-network support in Xaman, and a hackathon-provided devnet wallet would keep the real wallet for the final demo only.

## 5. Infrastructure, SDK and event tooling

On the venue Wi-Fi, TLS to ports 51233/51234 stalled while 443 worked; a port-443 reverse proxy would remove a class of first-hour failures. `new Client(url, { timeout })` sets the request timeout while the 5-second connect timeout is `connectionTimeout`, named only in the error. Depositing a fresh account's entire 1,000 XRP fails `tecINSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` (`90E991C72D…`) when the cause is the reserve for the new `MPToken`; a `tecINSUFFICIENT_RESERVE` or an SDK pre-check would say so. The workshop deck's tutorial link (`xrpl.org/docs/tutorials/lending`) is a 404. On the event's tooling: the DevEx hook tags `grep` and `cat` over documentation with a `tx_type` (28 rows in one session named `LoanSet` or `LoanPay` with nothing built), which will inflate attempt counts unless tagging is tied to a result code or a submit; and "max 3 pages" has no meaning for a markdown file, a word budget would.

## Conclusion

XLS-65 and XLS-66 handle the money mechanics of a bond remarkably well: subordination through first-loss cover, continuous yield through the price per share, guardrails we could demonstrate rather than describe. What they lack is time and identity: a signer that only counts after a date, a vault that only lends to one party, a redemption window the owner can set. Each is a small field on `SignerListSet`, `LoanBrokerSet` or `VaultSet`, and each would let a product like this drop an off-chain daemon or a backend-held key. On the tooling side, one adapter fix in `xrpl-connect` is what stands between custom networks and real wallets.
