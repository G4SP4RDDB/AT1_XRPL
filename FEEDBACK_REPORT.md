# Developer Feedback Report — XRPL Lending Protocol Hackathon 2026

**Track** 1, open-ended Single Asset Vault + Lending Protocol V1 · **Flavour** Loaded (XLS-65/66 + native 2-of-2 multisig used as a call-date enforcer) · **Environment** Custom Hackathon Devnet, `rippled 3.4.0-rc1`, NetworkID 4001 · **Libraries** `xrpl.js 5.2.0`, `xrpl-connect 0.8.2` · **Team** BSA Degen (`sunny-puffin-57`)

## What we tried to build, and how it went

We wanted to put an AT1 bond on the ledger: an issuer posts the terms, investors fund a vault, the loan goes out the moment the vault is full, coupons raise the price per share so investors can skim their yield whenever they like, and the principal stays locked until the call date. Two days later we have all of that running on the devnet, with seventeen lifecycle steps verified on-chain (six of them are deliberate rejections we are proud of, see `docs/e2e-full-report.md`). The protocol carried the money side beautifully. Where it made us work was time and identity: *when* something may happen, and *who* is allowed to be on the other side of a loan. Below is what we hit, roughly in the order we hit it, with what we would change. The full log with repros, severities and versions is `docs/friction-log.md`, 32 entries.

## 1. The thing we could not make native: a lock with a date on it

The whole product rests on one rule: nobody touches the principal before the call date. Half of it comes for free. While the money is out on loan, a full `VaultWithdraw` fails with `tecINSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` (tx `C4240633AC…`), and we happily demo that. The other half does not exist: nothing stops the issuer from repaying early with `tfLoanFullPayment`, which would refill the vault and let everyone leave. So we put the issuer's account under a 2-of-2 signer list (`SignerListSet`, then `AccountSet asfDisableMaster`) where the second signer is a small daemon we call the enforcer. It reads the validated ledger close time and simply refuses to co-sign a full payment before the date. The ledger does its part perfectly, a payment from the disabled master key dies with `tefMASTER_DISABLED` and a single-signature `LoanPay` with `tefBAD_QUORUM`, but the *date* lives in our daemon's code, because a `SignerEntry` has no idea what time it is.

Then we discovered we needed the same trick on the other side. The spec is explicit that the vault owner has no say over withdrawals (`VaultWithdraw` "does not respect the permissioned domain rules"; `AssetsMaximum` and `tfVaultPrivate` only gate deposits), so to keep an investor's principal in we run a second 2-of-2 on investor accounts and let the enforcer sign yield-share redemptions only. Two off-chain trust assumptions for one bond. What would have made both disappear: an optional `SignAfter` / `SignBefore` on `SignerEntry`, rejected by the engine with its own `tef` code, and an owner-set redemption window on `VaultSet` (`WithdrawalCapPerPeriod`, `WithdrawalPeriodSeconds`), which is just how real funds gate liquidity. We looked at `TokenEscrow` (enabled here, and buildable) but the escrow release and the `LoanPay` stay two unrelated transactions, which is exactly the gap a signer condition closes.

## 2. Things the ledger does differently from what we read

Our first evening was a series of small "oh". There is no `LoanDraw`; `LoanSet` moves the money itself. The spec (§3.8.6, item 6) says origination books `InterestDue` into `AssetsTotal`; the ledger does not: after `LoanSet` `23AA66A740…` the vault's `AssetsTotal` sat at 200,000,000 drops while the loan showed `TotalValueOutstanding` 200,000,762, and the number only moved when the first coupon landed. We had built our dashboard on the spec sentence and rebuilt it that night. Setting `AssetsMaximum` to exactly the amount raised makes `LoanSet` fail with `tecLIMIT_EXCEEDED`, because the cap has to hold the interest too, and the code does not say which cap you tripped. Rates are tenths of a basis point per year with a 60-second minimum interval, so a ten-minute demo loan earns fractions of a drop and small principals die of `tecPRECISION_LOSS`; a paragraph of worked devnet numbers, or a helper that computes `PeriodicPayment` and `InterestDue` for you, would save every team an afternoon.

Closing the loan taught us the most, partly at our own expense. Once a coupon is overdue you must set `tfLoanLatePayment`, which cannot be combined with `tfLoanFullPayment`, so a late loan cannot be settled in one go (our first try came back `tecEXPIRED`). The three `LoanPay` flags are tabulated nowhere with their values (`tfLoanOverpayment 0x10000`, `tfLoanFullPayment 0x20000`, `tfLoanLatePayment 0x40000`); we published wrong ones in an earlier draft of this very report. And nobody tells you that a "final repayment" at the call date is not a full payment at all, it is just the last coupon, after which the loan closes by itself; `tfLoanFullPayment` only ever means an early close. Our own settlement transaction (`EA805215F3…`) carries the late-payment flag, and we only understood why after reading the enum and the ledger. Fee destinations are scattered across three sections (the early-close penalty goes to the vault, PPS jumped 1.0000056 → 1.0066; `ClosePaymentFee` goes to the broker; `ManagementFeeRate` skims each coupon). `InterestRate` is frozen for the life of the loan, while an AT1 coupon resets at the call date; and every instalment amortises principal, there is no interest-only schedule with a bullet at maturity, which is what a bond coupon is (our first coupon modal let the issuer type 45 XRP and the ledger took 333, principal included). Impairment surprised us twice: with `fixCleanup3_4_0`, `tfLoanImpair` on a current loan is `tecTOO_SOON` (`E69143C182…`), so a write-down is not the discretionary broker action the workshop described, and `tfLoanUnimpair` on a healthy loan is `tecNO_PERMISSION` (`3CA1EDC1FD…`), which sends you looking for a signing bug. Finally, a teammate looked at our own "top up first-loss capital" button and asked what it did; the honest answer took four spec sections to assemble. One lifecycle paragraph next to `LoanBrokerCoverDeposit` would do.

## 3. Identity: a bond vault should lend to one party, and the ledger cannot say so

We assumed a vault could be tied to its issuer. It cannot: neither `VaultCreate` nor `LoanBrokerSet` can restrict the `Counterparty` of a `LoanSet`. We store the issuer in the vault's `Data` field and check it twice, in our shim and, independently, in the enforcer before it counter-signs. The odd part is that the *other* side does have a whitelist: `tfVaultPrivate` plus a `DomainID` restricts `VaultDeposit` to Credential holders (both amendments are enabled here, and it is exactly how an accredited-investor rule would be written; we ran out of hours before building it). Borrowers get nothing, and the depositor gate stops at deposits. A `DomainID` on `LoanBrokerSet`, or an `AllowedCounterparty`, would make our two checks a ledger rule. Related gaps in the same family: there is no "vault funded" event, so we originate by decoding the deposit blob after submission; an ask's funding window is off-chain, so a background scan settles stalled bonds at their deadline; and the bid / accept / decline layer we built on the last morning had to go, because a lender's "locked" bid was only a row in our database: `VaultDeposit` is immediate and nothing can reserve funds against an ask (`TokenEscrow` again: lock the bid, `EscrowFinish` on accept, `EscrowCancel` on expiry). Funding is now a direct deposit from the bond list. One more lesson learned the hard way: when our broker seed spent a day in a public README we found that a vault owner or loan broker key cannot be rotated, `VaultCreate` and `LoanBrokerSet` bind to the account. Please tell teams to set a RegularKey and a SignerList on day one, and put a seed grep in the starter kit's pre-commit hook; the DevEx hook already knows the patterns.

## 4. Wallets: the part that nearly did not work

We decided that everyone, investors, issuers and the broker, would sign with a real wallet over WalletConnect. It took three fixes, all against `xrpl-connect 0.8.2`, read in the minified bundle at five in the morning. The adapter identifies networks by CAIP id and only knows `xrpl:0/1/2`; ours has none. Pairing asks for `xrpl:2`, WalletConnect v2 quietly turns that into an optional namespace, Xaman approves the chains it supports, and every request we send is thrown out client-side, before the wallet sees it: `Failed to sign transaction. Missing or invalid. request() chainId: xrpl:2`. Then `sign()` turned out to return `tx_json.TxnSignature` as `tx_blob`, the bare signature, which nothing can submit, with `autofill: true` hardcoded. Then `reconnect()` turned out to never resume a session, it just proposes a new pairing nobody can approve, so a page reload logs you out. Our fix is small and ugly: we call the WalletConnect client ourselves with a chain id the wallet actually approved, `autofill: false` because our backend already filled everything including `NetworkID 4001`, and `encode(tx_json)` for the blob. After that, Xaman signed for our network without a complaint. The adapter could do all of this itself: read the chain from the session, expose `autofill`, return an encoded blob, resume sessions, and adopt an `xrpl:<NetworkID>` convention so custom networks can exist.

Two things we could not work around. No adapter can produce a multisig `Signers` entry (the WalletConnect adapter even asks for `xrpl_signTransactionFor` at pairing, then never calls it), so the operator key of every 2-of-2 account is generated and held by our backend, with the owner's wallet signing only the one-time `SignerListSet` + `AccountSet`. Worse, even a wallet that did implement `xrpl_signTransactionFor` could not sign a `LoanSet` as counterparty: `signLoanSetByCounterparty` uses its own hash prefixes (`0x43505400`, `0x43504d00`), and the spec never says so. That option, given as a string, is also used verbatim as `Signer.Account`, which is how we met "Duplicate Signers not allowed". Two adapter primitives, `signForMultisig()` and `signLoanSetAsCounterparty()`, plus one sentence in XLS-66 on what the counterparty signature signs, would let an issuer keep every key. And a small trap with a clear code: `autofill()` gives a transaction twenty ledgers to live, about a minute here; approving two transactions in a row on a phone takes longer, and the first one came back `tefMAX_LEDGER`. An offset option would do.

Beyond the bugs, we want to say plainly that developing against a phone wallet on a custom devnet is exhausting. Every signature is scan, tap, wait. Every reload is a new QR. Xaman cannot be pointed at this network, so it shows a testnet chain id, no balance and no history, and every test account is a seed typed in by hand from a text file. A rehearsal of the demo takes ten minutes instead of one. A teammate wrote a local-seed adapter to escape it; we removed it to keep a single honest wallet path, which was the right product call and the wrong developer experience. A clearly labelled developer signer in `xrpl-connect`, custom-network support in Xaman, or a hackathon wallet pre-configured for the devnet would let teams keep the real wallet for the final demo only.

## 5. Small things that cost real minutes

The venue Wi-Fi dropped TLS on ports 51233 and 51234 while 443 (the faucet) was fine; a port-443 proxy for the devnet would spare every team its first hour. `new Client(url, { timeout })` is the request timeout, the connect timeout is `connectionTimeout`, and you learn that from the error. Depositing a fresh account's whole 1,000 XRP fails `tecINSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` (`90E991C72D…`) when the real problem is the reserve for the new `MPToken`; a `tecINSUFFICIENT_RESERVE` would point straight at it. The workshop deck's tutorial link (`xrpl.org/docs/tutorials/lending`) is a 404. On the event's own tooling, two notes: the DevEx hook tagged our `grep` and `cat` over documentation with a `tx_type` (28 rows in one session named `LoanSet` or `LoanPay` with nothing built), so attempt counts will be inflated unless tagging is tied to a result code; and "max 3 pages" has no meaning for a markdown file, a word budget would.

## What we would ask for, if we could ask for three things

A signer that only counts after a date. A vault, or a broker, that only lends to one party. And one fix in `xrpl-connect` so a real wallet can sign on a custom network. XLS-65 and XLS-66 already do the hard part, subordination, continuous yield, guardrails we could demonstrate instead of describe. These three would let a product like ours drop its daemon and its backend-held key, and they are each one field.
