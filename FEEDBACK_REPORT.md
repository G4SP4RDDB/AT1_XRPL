# Developer Feedback Report — XRPL Lending Protocol Hackathon 2026

**Track**: 1, open-ended Single Asset Vault + Lending Protocol V1 · **Flavour**: Loaded (XLS-65/66 + native 2-of-2 multisig used as a call-date enforcer) · **Environment**: Custom Hackathon Devnet, `rippled 3.4.0-rc1`, NetworkID 4001, reserves 10 + 2 XRP · **Libraries**: `xrpl.js 5.2.0`, `xrpl-connect 0.8.2` · **Team**: BSA Degen (`sunny-puffin-57`)

## What we built, in one paragraph

An AT1 bond platform where one bond is one open-ended vault (`VaultCreate`), investors deposit through their own wallet (`VaultDeposit`), the deposit that funds the vault automatically originates the loan to the issuer (`LoanSet`), coupons (`LoanPay`) raise the price per share, investors harvest yield at any time with a partial `VaultWithdraw`, and principal stays locked until the call date. Seventeen steps of that lifecycle, including six deliberate rejections, are verified on the devnet (`docs/e2e-full-report.md`). Everything below was hit while building it; the raw log with repros, severities and library versions is `docs/friction-log.md` (30 entries). Every point ends with what we would change.

## 1. The headline: multisig has no notion of time

An AT1 bond needs the principal locked until the call date. XLS-65 gives half of that for free: while the principal is out on loan, a full `VaultWithdraw` fails with `tecINSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` (tx `C4240633AC…`). The other half is missing: nothing in XLS-66 stops the issuer from repaying early with `tfLoanFullPayment`, which would make the principal liquid before the date. We closed the gap with a 2-of-2 signer list on the issuer's account (`SignerListSet`, master key disabled with `AccountSet asfDisableMaster`): one key is the issuer's operator, the other belongs to an autonomous enforcer daemon that reads the validated ledger close time and refuses to co-sign a full payment before the call date. The ledger enforces the *who* faithfully (`tefMASTER_DISABLED` for the disabled key, `tefBAD_QUORUM` for a single signature, both in the e2e run), but the *when* lives in our daemon's policy, because a `SignerEntry` has no time condition. This is a trust assumption dressed as a lock. We propose an optional `SignAfter` / `SignBefore` ripple-epoch field on `SignerEntry`, rejected by the engine with a dedicated `tef` code, which would make fixed-maturity debt fully native. The composition with `TokenEscrow` (`FinishAfter = callDate`) is buildable today, we confirmed the amendment is enabled on this devnet, but escrow release and `LoanPay` remain two unlinked transactions, which is exactly what a signer condition would fix.

The depositor side has the same shape of problem. XLS-65 gives the vault owner no lever over withdrawals: the spec says `VaultWithdraw` "does not respect the permissioned domain rules", any shareholder can withdraw at will bounded only by `AssetsAvailable`, and `AssetsMaximum` / `tfVaultPrivate` gate deposits only. To keep an investor's principal locked until the call date we therefore run a second 2-of-2 on investor accounts, co-signed by the same enforcer, whose `decideWithdraw()` only signs redemptions within the accrued-yield shares until the loan is settled. An optional owner-set redemption gate on `VaultSet` (`WithdrawalCapPerPeriod` as an amount or a basis-point share of `AssetsTotal`, plus `WithdrawalPeriodSeconds`, enforced at `VaultWithdraw` time) would mirror the quarterly liquidity windows of real fund and bond structures and remove that second off-chain trust assumption entirely; it generalizes to any vault that needs redemption throttling.

## 2. Vaults and loans: what the ledger does versus what we expected

Three expectations broke on the first day. There is no `LoanDraw`: `LoanSet` originates and disburses in one transaction (the model does not exist in `xrpl.js`), which the overview material should say up front. The spec (§3.8.6 item 6) says origination increases `AssetsTotal` by `InterestDue`; on this devnet it does not: for `LoanSet` `23AA66A740…` the vault's `AssetsTotal` stayed at 200,000,000 drops while the loan was created with `TotalValueOutstanding` 200,000,762, and `AssetsTotal` only moved when the first coupon landed. We had built our PPS dashboard on the spec text and had to redo it; the spec should match the implementation or mark the change by amendment. Setting `AssetsMaximum` to the amount raised makes `LoanSet` fail with `tecLIMIT_EXCEEDED`, because the cap must also hold the interest headroom; the code does not say which cap failed, and a distinct code or an `InterestDueHeadroom` in `vault_info` would.

Two more came from closing the loan. Interest rates are tenths of a basis point per year with a 60-second minimum interval, so a ten-minute demo loan earns fractions of a drop and small principals hit `tecPRECISION_LOSS`; a "testing on devnet" note with worked numbers, or an SDK helper computing `PeriodicPayment` and `InterestDue`, would save every team an afternoon. Once a coupon is overdue, `LoanPay` requires `tfLoanLatePayment`, which is mutually exclusive with `tfLoanFullPayment` (the validator says so), so a delinquent loan cannot be settled in one transaction and our first attempt came back `tecEXPIRED`; letting a full payment sweep the overdue amounts would remove a whole state from every client. Related, and our own mistake: the three `LoanPay` flags are tabulated nowhere with their values (`tfLoanOverpayment 0x10000`, `tfLoanFullPayment 0x20000`, `tfLoanLatePayment 0x40000`), and nothing says that at the final due date you simply pay the last coupon and the loan closes by itself, `tfLoanFullPayment` only ever meaning an early close. We published wrong values and mislabelled our own settlement transaction (`EA805215F3…` carries `0x40000`, a late coupon) until we read the enum and the ledger. Where each fee goes is also scattered: the early close sent the `CloseInterestRate` penalty to the vault (PPS 1.0000056 → 1.0066) and the `ClosePaymentFee` to the broker, `ManagementFeeRate` skims each coupon; one "fee destinations" table would do.

Impairment surprised us twice. With `fixCleanup3_4_0`, `LoanManage tfLoanImpair` on a current loan returns `tecTOO_SOON` (tx `E69143C182…`), so a write-down is strictly an overdue-triggered action, not the discretionary broker call the workshop described; and `tfLoanUnimpair` on a healthy loan returns `tecNO_PERMISSION` (tx `3CA1EDC1FD…`), which reads as an authentication failure. Document the precondition and return a "not impaired" code. Finally, first-loss capital has no plain-language lifecycle anywhere: a team member asked what "top up the first-loss capital" meant, and the answer (the broker posts XRP with `LoanBrokerCoverDeposit`, it absorbs losses before depositors, `LoanSet` is refused while `CoverAvailable` is below `CoverRateMinimum × DebtTotal`, the broker refills it) had to be assembled from four places.

## 3. Two things a bond vault needs that the ledger cannot express

A bond vault must lend to its issuer and nobody else, and the loan should follow the funding automatically. Neither `VaultCreate` nor `LoanBrokerSet` can restrict the `Counterparty` of a `LoanSet`; the only per-vault place to record the issuer is the free-form `Data` field, where we store the bid. We enforce the rule in the shim and, independently, in the enforcer before it counter-signs. An `AllowedCounterparty` on `LoanBrokerSet`, or a required Credential type for borrowers, would make it a ledger rule. The asymmetry is striking once you look for it: the depositor side *does* have a native whitelist, `VaultCreate` with `tfVaultPrivate` and a `DomainID` restricts `VaultDeposit` to holders of the domain's Credentials (both amendments are enabled on this devnet), which is exactly how the real-world accredited-investor and minimum-ticket rules of an AT1 would be expressed; we did not get to it in 36 hours. The borrower side has nothing, `LoanBrokerSet` carries no `DomainID`, and the depositor gate stops at deposits since `VaultWithdraw` "does not respect the permissioned domain rules". A `DomainID` on `LoanBrokerSet`, symmetrical to the vault's, plus a flag letting a private vault's withdrawals and share transfers respect the domain, would give a regulated issuance both whitelists on-ledger. Automatic origination we do by decoding the deposit blob after submission and checking the vault state, since there is no "vault funded" hook. A bid's funding window is off-chain in the same way: nothing on the ledger expires a vault that never filled, so a background scan originates a partially funded bond at its deadline (above a 50 % floor) or leaves it for depositors to withdraw; a deposit deadline on `VaultCreate` after which `VaultDeposit` is refused would make the window a ledger fact. And when our broker seed leaked into a README for a day, we learned that a compromised vault owner or loan broker key cannot be rotated: `VaultCreate` and `LoanBrokerSet` bind to the submitting account, so the only remedy was regenerating everything. Documenting RegularKey + SignerList for vault owners from day one, and a pre-commit seed guard in the starter kit (the DevEx hook already has the redaction patterns), would prevent both the leak and the loss.

## 4. Wallets: `xrpl-connect` on a custom network

We chose WalletConnect (Xaman) as the only way to sign. It took three fixes, all against `xrpl-connect 0.8.2`, read in the shipped bundle. First, the adapter identifies the ledger by a CAIP-2 id and only knows `xrpl:0/1/2`; this network has none. Pairing asks for `xrpl:2`, WalletConnect v2 downgrades required namespaces to optional, the wallet approves the chains it supports, and every request tagged `xrpl:2` is rejected client-side, before the wallet sees it: `Failed to sign transaction. Missing or invalid. request() chainId: xrpl:2`. Second, `WalletConnectAdapter.sign()` hardcodes `autofill: true` and returns `tx_json.TxnSignature` as `tx_blob`, the bare signature, which cannot be submitted. Third, `reconnect()` never resumes the stored session; it proposes a new pairing nobody can approve, so nothing survives a page reload. Our fix is a `signPrepared()` helper that calls the WalletConnect client directly with a chain id taken from the approved session, `autofill: false` (our backend already autofilled, `NetworkID 4001` included), and `encode(tx_json)`. With that, Xaman signed `NetworkID 4001` transactions without complaint. The adapter should pick the request chain from the session namespaces, expose `autofill`, return `encode(tx_json)`, and restore sessions from SignClient storage; a `xrpl:<NetworkID>` convention would let custom networks exist at all.

Two structural gaps remain. No adapter can produce a multisig `Signers` entry (the WalletConnect adapter requests `xrpl_signTransactionFor` at pairing but never calls it), so each 2-of-2 account's operator key is generated and held by our backend, with the owner's wallet signing only the one-time `SignerListSet` + `AccountSet`. And even a wallet implementing `xrpl_signTransactionFor` could not sign a `LoanSet` as counterparty: `signLoanSetByCounterparty` uses dedicated hash prefixes (`0x43505400` single, `0x43504d00` multi, `fixCleanup3_4_0`), which the spec does not mention. We propose `signForMultisig()` and `signLoanSetAsCounterparty()` adapter primitives, and a sentence in XLS-66 stating what the counterparty signature signs. A smaller SDK point: `signLoanSetByCounterparty`'s `multisign` option, given as a string, is used verbatim as `Signer.Account`, so passing the borrower's address produced "Duplicate Signers not allowed"; the boolean is the right form and the docs should say so.

Last, a timing footgun with a clear code. `client.autofill()` sets `LastLedgerSequence` to the current ledger plus 20, about a minute here. The 2-of-2 activation needs two consecutive approvals in Xaman, so the first transaction expired and came back `tefMAX_LEDGER`, which our UI unhelpfully rendered as "multisig refused". An `autofill` option for the ledger offset, and a note that the default window is sized for immediate submission, would help anyone whose signer is a human.

Beyond the defects, signing through a mobile wallet on a custom devnet is simply impractical for iterative work. Every signature is a phone round trip (scan, approve, wait for the relay), the session does not survive a page reload so each reload means a new scan, the 2-of-2 activation is two approvals in a row, and Xaman cannot be pointed at this network at all: it shows the transaction under a testnet chain id, cannot display the account's real balance or history, and every test account has to be created by our script and its seed typed into the app by hand. A full lifecycle through the UI is five or six phone interactions; a rehearsal takes ten minutes instead of one. Our teammate had written a local-seed adapter to escape this and we removed it to keep one real-wallet path, which is the right product decision and the wrong development experience. A first-class, clearly labelled *developer signer* in `xrpl-connect` (seed or keypair held in the tab), custom-network support in Xaman (node URL + NetworkID), and a hackathon-provided wallet pre-configured for the devnet would let teams keep the real wallet for the final demo only.

## 5. Infrastructure, SDK and event tooling

On the venue Wi-Fi, TLS handshakes to ports 51233 and 51234 stalled while port 443 (the faucet) worked; a port-443 reverse proxy for the devnet would remove a whole class of first-hour failures. In `xrpl.js`, `new Client(url, { timeout })` sets the request timeout while the 5-second connect timeout is `connectionTimeout`, named only in the error text. Depositing a fresh account's entire 1,000 XRP fails `tecINSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` (tx `90E991C72D…`) when the real cause is the reserve for the new `MPToken` object; a `tecINSUFFICIENT_RESERVE` or an SDK pre-check would say so. The workshop deck's tutorial link (`xrpl.org/docs/tutorials/lending`) returns 404. XLS-66 has no rate reset: `InterestRate` is immutable for the life of the loan, while AT1 coupons reset at the call date; an oracle-driven reset or a `LoanBrokerRateReset` would be needed for real fixed income. On the event's own tooling: the DevEx hook tags `grep` and `cat` over documentation with a `tx_type` (28 rows in our session named `LoanSet` or `LoanPay` with no transaction built), which will inflate attempt counts unless tagging is tied to a result code or a submit; and "max 3 pages" has no meaning for a markdown file, a word budget would.

## Conclusion

XLS-65 and XLS-66 handle the money mechanics of a bond remarkably well: subordination through first-loss cover, continuous yield through the price per share, and clean guardrails that we could demonstrate rather than describe. What they lack is time and identity: a signer that only counts after a date, and a vault that only lends to one party. Both are small additions to `SignerListSet` and `LoanBrokerSet`, and both would let a product like this drop its off-chain daemon and its backend-held key. On the tooling side, one adapter fix in `xrpl-connect` (session-approved chain id, encoded `tx_blob`) is what stands between custom networks and real wallets.
